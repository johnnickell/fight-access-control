# Recoverable Agent Credential Operations

## Destination

An authorized consumer can retry Agent provisioning or rotation after a lost response and recover delivery of that
operation's original credential to its originally authorized protected destination, without another Agent, another
rotation, or unrestricted secret retrieval. AccessControl owns operation correlation, credential/delivery invariants,
and Application orchestration; consumers own authorization policy, persistence adapters, keys, protected storage,
and runtime.

### D2 — Transactional authorization and protected-sink integration

**Ratified by John on 2026-09-27; depends on D1:** accept the proposal's same-connection transactional
local-authority constraint and protected-sink protocol as mandatory supported-integration requirements, not advisory
consumer guidance.

- Consumer policy owns caller, target, delegation and destination decisions. A consumer-implemented injected
  capability participates in the **package-owned** Unit of Work, reads authoritative records and holds their
  version/authorization fences until commit; it neither begins nor commits a transaction. Every authority writer,
  revocation, reassignment and package lifecycle path must share those fences, including direct non-HTTP calls.
  No actor string, supplied allow boolean, cached external-provider decision or nested outer transaction substitutes.
  External-provider consumers need an authoritative local grant participating in this protocol, or remain unsupported
  pending a separately accepted and qualified alternative.
- A claim reserves work only. A short transaction authorizes sensitive admission for the current claim, invoking
  principal/delegation, original scope, exact active credential and destination binding/order, retention and trusted
  time. **Successful admission commit is the linearization point.** Persist revisions/epochs and the earliest claim,
  authorization and delivery deadline. Uncertain admission never permits materialization/invocation; resolve it and
  re-admit when needed. Recheck the recorded deadline after commit and before materialization/invocation.
- Revocation/reassignment/supersession before admission denies without materialization. After admission, an in-flight
  call may finish only at the fixed sink and stage inert bytes; no cross-system rollback or recall promise is made.
  Acknowledgement freshly validates claim, admission epochs/expiry, current credential and destination. Revoke/regrant
  cannot validate an old admission. New authorized claims may reconcile receipts; old claimants cannot acknowledge.
- Accept immutable versioned sink staging, globally unique delivery-ID idempotency, exact opaque durable receipt
  binding, monotonic per-slot write order/high-water marks and persistent deduplication tombstones. A receipt is not
  permission to activate or a secret-read handle. Sink arrival order cannot select usable credentials. Unsupported
  sinks fail closed; no generic secret-store put interface is sufficient without these guarantees.
- Consumer enrollment activation requires confirmed delivery of the exact tuple and fresh transactional authority;
  the broker checks current use and exact credential/destination authority at each use. Late bytes/receipts cannot
  activate, select, revive or authorize an obsolete credential. Consumer implementation remains outside this EPIC.

The accepted public representation direction is typed, non-serializable sensitive invocation plus typed sanitized
outcome/opaque receipt and secret-free status. Exact PHP names/signatures and concrete SQL/lock ordering belong to
later accepted requirements/design; they must fit Domain/Application ownership and cannot weaken these fences.
This is a narrow credential-operation capability, not restoration of retired actor-only Permission-admin ports or
package ownership of consumer Permission names.

### D3 — Post-commit result behavior

**Ratified by John on 2026-09-27; depends on D1–D2:** for the replacement recoverable Agent provisioning and
rotation boundary, return **confirmed committed operation metadata with a typed, sanitized publication warning**
when post-commit publication fails, rather than throwing for that publication failure. Pre-commit failures remain
failures; an uncertain commit is a distinct safe indeterminate outcome, never a claimed rollback or committed
success. Durable operation and delivery records, not success/failure events, determine the outcome. Exact public
PHP names remain for later design; the warning must be typed and cannot expose arbitrary exception/provider detail.

Keep same-key resolution/status available after response loss, publisher failure or restart. Even if failure-event
publication also fails, preserve committed issuance and pending delivery work and let authorized scheduled discovery
recover it without caller retry. Retries do not create another mutation or audit fact. Notification retry, if supported,
retains stable fact identity and documented duplicate semantics. Never include secret-bearing results or arbitrary
publisher exceptions in safe outputs/events. This does not introduce a generic event bus/outbox framework.

**Committed issuance is not confirmed credential delivery, enrollment activation or permission to launch.** Each
requires its own confirmed outcome and current authorization. A publication warning is neither delivery evidence nor
an authorization capability; no caller, scheduler or broker may use committed issuance alone to infer readiness.

This is an explicit exception to blanket post-commit failure/rethrow guidance **only for replacement recoverable
Agent provision and rotation**. It does not change revocation, AuthenticationService, other CommandHandlers, or the
existing raw-return implementation. The project profile, CONTEXT and dated ADR 0004/WF-002 amendments record this
scope; implementation, release and adoption remain separate.

Before implementation acceptance, require the proposed failure/restart tests for both operations: pre-commit
rollback, both committed and rolled-back uncertain commits, response loss/restart and exact same-key resolution,
post-commit publication failure (including both publishers failing), and authorized scheduler-only restart recovery
without caller retry. Assert persisted outcomes, typed sanitized warnings only for confirmed commits, distinct
indeterminate results, single mutation/audit facts, and no inferred delivery, activation or launch permission.
Consumer activation/use authorization proof remains required in real consumer conformance, not inferred from package
unit tests. This ratification is not a test receipt.

### D4 — Bounded operation and integration policy

**Ratified with amendment by John on 2026-09-27; depends on D1–D3:** provide documented, finite defaults and
optional validated consumer overrides for the limits needed to prevent runaway credential-operation work and
preserve correct recovery. This replaces the earlier mandatory-configuration recommendation. Cover input sizes,
outstanding-operation admission, claim/admission duration, retries, delivery retention and inert-sink cleanup with
validated relationships; do not introduce a general-purpose quota or approval framework.

Routine operation and recovery require neither manual configuration nor an additional human approval step. Omitting
overrides selects the documented defaults; invalid overrides reject safely rather than disabling limits. Current
caller/delegation/destination authorization and D2's fences remain mandatory, but they do not imply a human approval
workflow. Exceptional terminal loss or ambiguous historical state still requires the reconciliation already specified;
that is not a routine recovery prerequisite.

Capacity exhaustion rejects or defers **new work** with a clear, safe retryable outcome, without partial issuance.
Preserve authorized safe status lookup, same-key resolution and recovery of existing operations; new-work admission
limits must not turn existing operations into fresh issuance or prevent their recovery. Keep recovery itself bounded
without routing routine retries through human approval. No automatic new key, secret regeneration, unsafe fallback
or eviction of required correlation evidence is allowed. This does not promise storage availability during an outage;
uncertain commits retain D3's distinct indeterminate outcome and same-key reconciliation.

Cleanup preserves enough secret-free operation, sink deduplication and ordering/high-water evidence to prevent
duplicate issuance or stale delivery. Expiry never makes an issued key reusable; delayed calls cannot recreate or
select retired material. Key retirement accounts for every pending copy. Finite secret/work retention does not
permit silently discarding the evidence needed for these guarantees.

Concrete default values, allowed override ranges, validation relationships, capacity/recovery scheduling and cleanup
mechanisms belong in requirement/design work before implementation acceptance, not this ratification. That work
must document and test default-only routine operation, valid/invalid overrides, bounded retries, capacity exhaustion
with existing-operation recovery, and cleanup/replay safety. Provider choice, consumer schemas and exact rollout
commands remain consumer-owned. No new human decision is required merely to use the defaults or recover normally.

## Accepted recovery invariants

1. **Request-bound identity:** trusted consumer/authority namespace plus authenticated caller type/stable ID scopes the
   caller-generated opaque operation ID retained before invocation. IDs/names are not capabilities. Package computes a
   versioned canonical request binding kind, normalized provision name or rotation target/predecessor, immutable
   registered destination identity/revision and outcome-affecting options. Same key/request resolves; changed binding
   conflicts without mutation/disclosure. Equal opaque IDs across scopes are independent. Every retry, no-op, status,
   discovery and recovery checks current authority; denial must not reveal another scope's existence.
2. **Atomic outcome:** one package Unit of Work on one shared connection commits operation/request, Agent mutation,
   credential tuple, encrypted delivery/reference, destination write reservation and safe audit. Concurrent identical
   attempts have one winner; uniqueness/CAS losers roll back then resolve it. Conflicts leave no second Agent,
   authoritative credential or audit. Capacity exhaustion rejects or defers new work with a clear retryable outcome
   and no partial issuance, while preserving authorized status and recovery of existing operations. Indeterminate
   commit is explicitly reconciled; unavailable storage never justifies guessing an outcome or using another key.
3. **Distinct identities:** immutable globally unique delivery UUID is the sink key **alone**, never a claim token or
   unscoped operation ID. Takeover advances claim fence/token/lease but keeps delivery ID and original bytes. Agent
   revisions order one Agent's credentials; destination versions order all writes across Agents/scopes to one slot.
   Issuance reserves that order under the slot ownership/reassignment fence; rebinding never resets its high-water mark.
4. **Exact sink binding:** idempotency and receipt bind delivery ID, Agent/credential ID/revision, destination identity/
   binding revision/write version and original bytes. Changed tuple/bytes, equal version with changed binding, or
   missing/mismatched/unverifiable receipt rejects. Public receipt/status has no secret digest, ciphertext, path,
   provider error or decryption handle. Old arrivals cannot overwrite/select/reactivate newer material; an identical
   retry may return its receipt without changing selection. Before the sink learns a newer package rotation, old
   accepted bytes remain inert because use selects the exact current package/destination authority, not arrival order.
5. **Separate secret lifetime:** integrity-bound delivery ciphertext is distinct from the authentication envelope.
   Materialization occurs only for a short-lived non-serializable sensitive invocation after confirmed admission;
   no raw/ciphertext credential in ordinary responses, debug output, messages, audit or events. Completion or terminal
   expiry/revocation/supersession retires the delivery copy, not the authentication envelope. Temporary key/storage
   failure retains ciphertext/key version; corruption, permanent key loss or exhausted retention fails safely for
   reconciliation. Rewrapping preserves bytes, binding and delivery ID. Never use the authentication envelope as fallback.
6. **Honest recovery:** at-least-once sink invocation, not exactly-once disclosure. Reconcile durable receipts before
   unnecessary materialization where supported. Lost sink responses/outcome commits reuse the same ID/bytes under
   current admission. After acknowledged delivery, recovery is safe receipt/status lookup, not renewed secret access.
   Sink loss cannot undo issuance, restore a predecessor, auto-rotate, emit raw fallback or mark enrollment ready.
7. **Safe status:** preserve original Agent/credential/revision and issuance result separately from current delivery/
   lifecycle disposition: pending, delivered, superseded, revoked, expired, retryable storage/key failure, terminal
   delivery failure and indeterminate outcome. Retired operations never resolve to a successor's secret. Safe tombstones
   prevent old keys becoming fresh issuance after delivery cleanup or retention expiry.

## Compatibility and migration requirements

- Existing Agents retain current authentication authority/envelopes; upgrade alone neither rotates nor redelivers.
  Mark missing historical correlation as legacy/non-recoverable. Never manufacture operation/request/delivery/receipt
  bindings from names/audit or copy authentication envelopes into delivery storage. A known active Agent needs an
  explicitly authorized new rotation for recovery; ambiguous old provisioning needs human reconciliation; revoked
  Agents stay terminal. Safe schema/version markers are not fabricated recovery outcomes.
- Inventory Agents, pending work, keys and every lifecycle/authorization writer; rehearse backup/restore using owning
  consumer tools. Quiesce/fence old issuance, rotation/revocation, delivery and authority writers, including admitted
